THE PAPER

Abstract

Robots operating in the real world often need to complete continuous tasks across indoor and outdoor spaces, such as moving from a living room through a porch onto the street. However, existing methods typically treat indoor and outdoor scene generation as separate tasks, making it difficult to generate unified and spatially continuous open environments. This stems from two core challenges: (1) Continuous indoor-outdoor scene data is scarce, so reliable joint generation priors are hard to obtain; (2) Indoor layouts and outdoor connections need to be coordinated, while post-hoc stitching often yields unnatural transitions.

To address these challenges, we propose WorldBridge, a procedural framework that bridges indoor and outdoor spaces to generate the unified open world. WorldBridge is built on three components. First, a Unified World Planner introduces explicit procedural priors to jointly organize indoor spaces, building boundaries, and outdoor environments. Second, a Collaborative Scene Generator jointly synthesizes aligned indoor, transitional, and outdoor regions under shared spatial and semantic constraints. Finally, an Executable Scene Builder bridges procedural generation and embodied simulation by converting object-level assets into semantically annotated, physically grounded, and dynamically configurable scenes.
